## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines the goals, structure, content direction, visual
approach, and functional requirements for the PineryZ corporate website.

It is intended to give designers, developers, and AI coding tools
(including Cursor) a shared and accurate understanding of the website to
be built.

This is a **corporate website**, not a VaultOS application, product
dashboard, token sale page, or speculative-asset marketing site.

The website should explain PineryZ as a technology company and introduce
VaultOS as its planned flagship product.

### Source-of-truth rule

Use `BUSINESS_OVERVIEW.md` for company facts and `PRODUCT_OVERVIEW.md`
for product facts. This document describes how those facts should be
presented on the website.

If information is missing from the source documents, do not invent it.
Mark it as an open question or omit it until the company owner supplies
and approves the information.

## 3. Website goals

The website should:

1.  Introduce PineryZ as a serious technology company.
2.  Communicate the company's vision and business direction.
3.  Explain the infrastructure challenges PineryZ aims to address.
4.  Introduce VaultOS as the planned flagship product.
5.  Explain the types of businesses PineryZ intends to serve.
6.  Establish credibility through clear, accurate, well-organized
    communication and professional design.
7.  Encourage potential business customers, partners, developers, and
    investors to contact PineryZ.

The primary conversion goal is to encourage relevant business inquiries.
Do not optimize the site around token purchases, retail trading, or
speculative investment.

## 5. Brand and visual direction

### Desired brand personality

PineryZ should feel:

-   Professional.
-   Innovative.
-   Trustworthy.
-   Technology-driven.
-   Modern.
-   Institutional.
-   Forward-looking.

### Visual approach

Use a hybrid corporate-fintech aesthetic:

-   Strong typographic hierarchy and generous spacing.
-   A restrained, coherent color system.
-   Clean layouts and clear content grouping.
-   Carefully selected abstract or technical visuals related to
    infrastructure, networks, vaults, and financial systems.
-   Product diagrams or interface concepts only when they clarify the
    product vision.
-   Subtle transitions and animations, with reduced-motion support.
-   Consistent visual treatment across desktop, tablet, and mobile.

### Design exploration proposals

The following are suggestions, not approved brand specifications:

-   Explore deep navy, charcoal, off-white, and a restrained blue, teal,
    or green accent.
-   Explore a modern sans-serif type family with strong readability.
-   Use abstract geometric forms, network structures, layered systems,
    or architectural motifs rather than generic cryptocurrency imagery.
-   Consider a mix of dark and light sections to create visual rhythm.

Do not use visual treatments that make PineryZ look like a meme coin,
token launch, trading exchange, casino, or consumer crypto wallet.

### Logo and brand assets

Use existing approved PineryZ and VaultOS assets if they are present in
the repository. Do not invent a final logo or alter supplied brand
assets without approval.

If approved assets are missing, use a neutral text-based placeholder and
record the missing asset as an open item.

## 7. Homepage requirements

The homepage should tell a coherent story in a deliberate sequence. The
following section order is recommended.

### 7.1 Header / navigation

Requirements:

-   Display the PineryZ brand.
-   Provide clear navigation to the main pages.
-   Include a prominent Contact Us action.
-   Support mobile navigation with an accessible menu.
-   Keep the header visually consistent with the overall design.
-   Do not make the header excessively tall or visually dominant.

### 7.2 Hero section

Purpose: Communicate what PineryZ is and the direction it is pursuing
within a few seconds.

Suggested headline direction:

**Building the Infrastructure for the Future of Finance.**

Suggested supporting message:

PineryZ is developing blockchain-based infrastructure designed to help
businesses build and manage next-generation financial products.

This is proposed website copy based on the business overview. It must
not imply that a complete production platform is already available.

Suggested call to action:

-   Explore VaultOS
-   Contact Us

Visual direction:

-   Strong headline and concise supporting copy.
-   A distinctive abstract infrastructure visual or approved brand
    artwork.
-   Clear visual hierarchy and sufficient contrast.
-   Avoid unsupported statistics, customer logos, or performance claims.

### 7.3 Company introduction

Purpose: Explain PineryZ's role and business direction.

Content should communicate:

-   PineryZ is a technology company.
-   Its focus includes blockchain infrastructure, financial technology,
    and Web3.
-   Its long-term vision is to make on-chain financial infrastructure
    more accessible, configurable, and easier for businesses to adopt.

Do not invent a founding story, founder biography, office location, or
company history.

### 7.4 The challenge / opportunity

Purpose: Explain the problem PineryZ aims to address.

Content direction:

Businesses seeking to build on-chain financial products may need to
assemble and integrate multiple parts of the infrastructure themselves.
PineryZ aims to make this process more accessible and configurable.

Present this as the company's problem framing and direction, not as a
proven market-research finding.

Possible themes:

-   Infrastructure complexity.
-   Need for configurable product foundations.
-   Difficulty of assembling a complete technical stack.
-   Need for infrastructure that can support different business models.

Avoid quantitative market claims unless separately researched and
approved.

### 7.5 VaultOS introduction

Purpose: Introduce the planned flagship product and explain its
relationship to PineryZ.

Content should communicate:

-   VaultOS is PineryZ's planned flagship product.
-   It is envisioned as white-label on-chain financial infrastructure.
-   Its primary intended direction is helping businesses create and
    manage on-chain financial vaults.
-   VaultOS is under development / at the product-vision stage.

Suggested CTA: **Explore VaultOS**

Use accurate status language. Do not present mockups as screenshots of a
functioning product.

### 7.6 Intended customer segments

Purpose: Help visitors understand which types of businesses the company
is considering serving.

Potential segments:

-   RWA platforms.
-   Digital asset managers.
-   DeFi companies.
-   Fintech companies.
-   Web3 businesses.

Use concise descriptions based on the product and business overviews.
Clearly treat these as intended customer segments, not existing
customers.

Do not display customer logos, testimonials, or case studies unless
supplied and approved.

### 7.7 Company approach / value themes

Potential themes, derived from the stated company and product direction:

-   Configurable infrastructure.
-   More accessible on-chain product development.
-   White-label product direction.
-   Infrastructure designed with business adoption in mind.

These should be expressed as design and product intentions, not
guarantees or verified performance claims.

### 7.8 Closing call to action

Purpose: Invite relevant businesses and potential partners to start a
conversation.

Suggested direction:

**Building something on-chain? Let's talk.**

Supporting copy should invite businesses interested in on-chain
financial infrastructure to contact PineryZ.

Do not promise response times or services that have not been confirmed.

### 7.9 Footer

Potential elements:

-   PineryZ name or approved logo.
-   Short company description.
-   Main navigation links.
-   Contact link or approved business email.
-   Privacy Policy and Terms links only when the corresponding pages
    exist and are approved.
-   Copyright notice using an approved company identity and publication
    details.

Do not invent a postal address, telephone number, email address, legal
entity name, or registration details.

## 9. VaultOS page requirements

The VaultOS page should introduce the product vision in more detail
while making its current status unmistakable.

### Recommended content structure

1.  **Product hero:** VaultOS name, category, concise explanation, and
    status language.
2.  **Product vision:** A configurable way for businesses to develop,
    launch, and manage on-chain financial vaults.
3.  **Intended capabilities:** Creating and managing on-chain vaults,
    with related financial products remaining subject to scope
    definition.
4.  **White-label direction:** Explain the intended white-label model
    without claiming that branding, deployment, APIs, or other specific
    options are already implemented.
5.  **Potential customers:** RWA platforms, digital asset managers,
    DeFi, fintech, and Web3 businesses.
6.  **Technical direction:** If included, label technologies as proposed
    or under consideration.
7.  **Contact CTA:** Invite relevant businesses to discuss the
    direction.

### Product status language

Preferred phrases:

-   "Under development."
-   "Product vision."
-   "Planned flagship product."
-   "Envisioned as..."
-   "Designed to help businesses..." (when clearly describing intended
    direction).

Do not use language such as "live now," "fully operational," "trusted by
leading institutions," or "production-ready" unless those statements
become true and are approved.

### Technical information

The product overview lists the following as proposals:

-   Base and other EVM-compatible networks.
-   Solidity.
-   ERC-4626.
-   Node.js and TypeScript.
-   PostgreSQL.
-   React and Next.js.
-   viem and wagmi.

If this information appears on the website, label it as the proposed
technical direction. Do not present it as a finalized stack or evidence
of an existing implementation.

## 10. Contact page and inquiry flow

The contact page should provide a straightforward way for potential
business customers and partners to contact PineryZ.

Recommended fields, subject to implementation and owner approval:

-   Name.
-   Work email.
-   Company.
-   Inquiry type.
-   Message.

Potential inquiry types:

-   Business inquiry.
-   Partnership.
-   Technical discussion.
-   Other.

Functional requirements:

-   Validate required fields.
-   Provide clear success and error feedback.
-   Include appropriate spam protection.
-   Protect submitted personal information.
-   Do not display a form that appears functional unless a real
    submission destination and backend workflow are configured.
-   Do not invent an email address or connect the form to an unapproved
    destination.

If no backend or approved contact destination exists yet, implement a
clearly documented placeholder or a simple approved contact method
rather than a fake submission flow.

-   Partnerships or integrations.

-   Completed security audits.
-   Assets under management or transaction volume.

-   Regulatory licenses or registrations.
-   Team members, founders, advisors, or offices.
-   Product availability or implemented features.
-   Performance, security, compliance, or financial guarantees.

When in doubt, omit the claim and add it to the open questions list.

## 12. UX and interaction requirements

The website should be easy to understand and navigate.

Requirements:

-   Clear page hierarchy and predictable navigation.
-   Visible calls to action.
-   Readable line lengths and comfortable spacing.
-   Consistent buttons, links, cards, and section patterns.
-   Meaningful hover, focus, active, and disabled states.
-   Keyboard-accessible navigation and controls.
-   Visible focus indicators.
-   Semantic page structure and appropriate heading order.
-   Accessible color contrast.
-   Respect for reduced-motion preferences.
-   No interaction that depends solely on hover.
-   Avoid unnecessary animations, parallax, or distracting effects.

Animations should be subtle and purposeful. They must not interfere with
reading, navigation, or performance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Responsive design requirements

The website must work across:

-   Desktop.
-   Laptop.
-   Tablet.
-   Mobile.

Requirements:

-   Use responsive layouts rather than fixed desktop-only dimensions.
-   Ensure navigation works on touch devices.
-   Prevent horizontal overflow.
-   Maintain readable typography at smaller sizes.
-   Stack multi-column content appropriately on mobile.
-   Ensure buttons and interactive controls have comfortable touch
    targets.
-   Optimize images and visual assets for different screen sizes.
-   Test representative viewport sizes before completion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Accessibility, performance, and SEO

### Accessibility

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA practices where reasonably applicable.

Include:

-   Semantic HTML.
-   Keyboard navigation.
-   Appropriate labels for form controls.
-   Descriptive link text.
-   Alternative text for meaningful images.
-   Sufficient color contrast.
-   Reduced-motion support.

### Performance

-   Optimize image formats and sizes.
-   Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
-   Minimize layout shifts.
-   Load non-critical assets efficiently.
-   Avoid excessive animation and large visual effects.
-   Test performance on mobile as well as desktop.

### SEO and metadata

Prepare:

-   Unique page titles and meta descriptions.
-   Semantic headings.
-   Descriptive URLs.
-   Open Graph metadata.
-   Appropriate favicon and brand metadata when assets are available.
-   Sitemap and robots configuration where appropriate.

Do not add fabricated structured data, reviews, ratings, organization
addresses, or social profiles.

## 15. Technical implementation guidance

The product overview proposes React and Next.js for the frontend,
Node.js and TypeScript for the backend, and PostgreSQL for the database.
These are product technical directions, not necessarily final decisions
for the corporate website.

For the corporate website:

-   Inspect the existing repository before choosing or changing
    frameworks.
-   Prefer the existing stack if it is suitable.
-   If the repository is empty, propose a maintainable frontend
    architecture and explain the recommendation before implementation.
-   Keep reusable UI components separate from page-specific content.
-   Keep design tokens and global styles organized.
-   Use maintainable routing and navigation.
-   Keep content easy to update.
-   Avoid adding blockchain wallet connections, smart-contract
    interactions, token functionality, or a product dashboard to the
    corporate website unless explicitly requested later.

Do not install dependencies, replace configuration, or rewrite existing
files without first explaining material changes and obtaining approval.

## 16. Suggested implementation phases

### Phase 1 --- Requirements and repository review

-   Read the business overview.
-   Read the product overview.
-   Read this website requirements document.
-   Inspect the current repository and available assets.
-   Identify missing information and technical constraints.
-   Produce an implementation plan.

### Phase 2 --- Design foundation

-   Confirm sitemap and page priorities.
-   Propose color, typography, spacing, and layout tokens.
-   Define reusable components.
-   Establish responsive and accessibility patterns.
-   Review the design direction before building all pages.

### Phase 3 --- Homepage

-   Implement the global header and footer.
-   Build homepage sections in the specified narrative order.
-   Add approved assets and restrained interactions.
-   Review desktop and mobile layouts.

### Phase 4 --- Supporting pages

-   Build About.
-   Build VaultOS.
-   Build Contact when the submission method is approved.

### Phase 5 --- Quality assurance

-   Test navigation and links.
-   Test forms if implemented.
-   Check responsive layouts.
-   Check accessibility basics.
-   Check loading performance and console errors.
-   Verify every factual claim against the source documents.

### Phase 6 --- Deployment preparation

-   Configure production environment settings.
-   Confirm domain and hosting details with the owner.
-   Configure analytics only if approved.
-   Verify metadata, sitemap, and robots configuration.
-   Complete a final content and functionality review before launch.

Cursor must not skip directly to implementation without first producing
a plan and obtaining the owner's approval.
